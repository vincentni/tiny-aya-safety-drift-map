Tiny Aya Global gives a harmful answer to 9% of harmful requests. Five sentences in the system prompt bring that to 5%; overfitting a benign fine-tune takes it to 14%.

Tiny Aya Global (3.35B) on 600 red-teaming prompts in six languages, with a system-prompt patch, two LoRA fine-tunes of the same benign rows and 4-bit quantization measured on the same map. One judge labels every answer under a fixed public rubric. Code and write-up.

Content warning. The study uses red-teaming prompts. The tables and figures show counts only. The replay explorer at the end of the page, when its file is present, shows prompt text, and model answers behind a click; the text of answers judged harmful is not shown.

The light recipe costs no safety, mostly because its answers are shorter and it refuses slightly more; among complete answers it is not safer.
