Tiny Aya Global answers most questions its passage cannot answer. A probe on its activations makes it abstain there.

Tiny Aya Global (3.35B) is asked questions about passages, half of which the passage cannot answer (SQuAD 2.0). A probe on its layer-20 hidden state, trained on the model's own errors, flags the answers it is about to get wrong; the model abstains where the probe fires and answers as before elsewhere. No weight changes. One instruction line in the prompt, with no probe, is the baseline it is set beside. Question pairs from BBQ and MBBQ, further down, are secondary evidence. Code and write-up.

Secondary evidence: who did it, when the text does not say?

A short text, a question about who did something, and three options: two people identified by a group, and "cannot be determined". An ambiguous item's text does not settle the answer; its twin adds the sentences that do. The items are from MBBQ, a published, curated version of BBQ in English, Spanish, Dutch and Turkish: 150 pairs per language, all of question types the fine-tune never trained on. The attribution fine-tune saw English rows from BBQ only. The light fine-tune is a benign fine-tune with the same settings: 1,272 rows in six languages, with no such question in them.

The same model on harmful requests

The side checks of the fine-tune use the base model's own map as their reference: 600 red-teaming prompts in six languages, with a system-prompt patch, two LoRA fine-tunes of the same benign rows, a weight-space repair and 4-bit quantization measured on it. One judge labels every answer under a fixed public rubric. Below, "fine-tuned model" is the light fine-tune.

The light recipe costs no safety, mostly because its answers are shorter and it refuses slightly more; among complete answers it is not safer.

A targeted system-prompt patch

Guidance on biased content and self-harm, added to the system turn below the template's safety paragraph. Each model is compared with itself under the served template, over the prompts the judge could label both ways. The guidance was written after the weak categories were seen on these 600 prompts, so the table is in-sample: on 600 held-out prompts from the same source the base model's harmful answers go from 50 of 599 (8.3%) to 37 of 597 (6.2%), about half the change shown here.
